## 1. Three trips look bad. None of them were bad trips.

The statement shows three trips well below the pack:

| Trip | Revenue | Cost | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8–9 July · J3-2604 | ৳144,900 | ৳135,731 | **৳9,169** |
| 15–16 July · J3-2607 | ৳140,000 | ৳100,189 | ৳39,811 |
| 29–30 July · J3-2613 | ৳149,500 | ৳106,627 | ৳42,873 |

Against a normal trip clearing ৳70–105k, these read like disasters. Open the sheets and they are not:

- **8–9 July** carries `Engine Repair (Materials + workshop)` — **৳59,950**. Strip it and the trip cost ৳75,781 and made ৳69,119. Perfectly ordinary.
- **15–16 July** carries fourteen one-off lines that are almost all asset purchase and refurbishment: Chair ৳6,200, Motor ৳7,800, Burnish Mistri ৳3,400, Burnish Materials ৳2,005, Silencer Screw ৳2,200, Fan ৳1,700, Exhaust Fan ৳1,550, Tea Flask ৳1,400, Color ৳1,400, Jalai Mistri ৳1,100, Pati ৳800, Screw Driver + Petrol ৳500, Water Pump Baring ৳240 — **≈ ৳30,295**. Without it: ৳69,894 cost, ৳70,106 profit.
- **29–30 July** carries Generator Mistri ৳1,200 and Generator Parts ৳5,630, **plus a Staff Bill of ৳41,000** against the ৳19,500–21,000 every other trip pays. That ~৳20,000 excess is the single largest unexplained figure in the month.

**≈ ৳97,000 of the month's ৳1,087,597 "trip cost" is maintenance and capital spending, not the cost of running a trip.** That is 9% of trip cost, and it lands on whichever trip happened to be sailing when the workshop invoice arrived.

### Why this matters more than it looks

Per-trip profit is the number used to judge trips — pricing, occupancy targets, which dates are worth running. Right now it says 8–9 July was a near-total loss. It wasn't; it was an average trip that paid for an engine.

There is already a **Renovation** line in the monthly statement (৳250,000 for July) and a separate renovation report. So capital spending has a home — but repair spending is being booked two different ways depending on who wrote it down.

**Recommendation:** add a repair/capital head to the cost sheet and exclude it from the per-trip profit figure, or book it to the renovation report as the ৳250,000 already is. Either is fine. Doing both inconsistently is not.

Adjusted for this, July's real trip operating performance was roughly **৳990,500 cost → ৳932,000 operating profit**, not ৳834,903.

## 2. Occupancy is the swing factor, and the worst trip was also the cheapest

| 10–11 July · WJ3-2605 | 27 | ৳178,000 | ৳6,593 |

Average 25.8 guests against a 36-berth boat — about **72% occupancy**.

The 19–20 July trip is the one to look at: fewest guests *and* the lowest rate per guest, ৳4,971 against ৳6,593 on the best trip. It still made ৳20,966, so it was worth sailing — but it was discounted into a thin margin rather than being a quiet weekend at normal rates.

Note the pattern in the trip IDs: the `WJ3-` trips (10–11, 24–25) carry the highest revenue per guest. Whatever distinguishes them is working and is worth naming explicitly.

**The floor to hold:** cost per person on a normal trip is roughly ৳2,950 once repairs are excluded. Below about ৳4,000 a head, a trip stops contributing meaningfully to marketing and renovation recovery.

## 4. Staff cost is stable — with one exception

Nine of thirteen trips paid ৳19,500–21,000 in Staff Bill. The salary sheet reconciles: ten staff, mostly trip-based (Sukani ৳3,000, Chef ৳3,500, Chef Assistant ৳2,000, Driver ৳3,000, In-charge ৳3,000, Service ×2 ৳1,500, Supervisor ৳1,500, Marketing ৳1,000, Manager ৳1,000/trip + ৳20,000 monthly), all 13 trips completed except the Supervisor at 8.

The **৳41,000 on 29–30 July** is the outlier — roughly double, and roughly the Manager's ৳20,000 monthly on top of a normal trip bill. If that is what it is, the monthly salary is being charged to one arbitrary trip, which distorts that trip exactly the way the engine repair distorts 8–9 July.

---

## What to change

1. **Separate repair and capital spend from trip operating cost.** Biggest single distortion in the month, ~৳97,000.
2. **Charge the Manager's monthly salary to the month, not to a trip.** Same problem, ~৳20,000.
3. **Split advertising from one-off builds** in the marketing report.
4. **Record why a trip was discounted.** 19–20 July at ৳4,971/guest may have been the right call; nothing in the paperwork says.
5. State on the statement whether cost-per-person includes marketing and renovation. It currently does, and the two readings differ by ৳1,005 a head.

## What this told us about the software

Two things were fixed in the plugin as a direct result of reading these sheets:

- **The 15–16 July sheet used fourteen one-off rows.** The cost sheet shipped with three. The spare rows are now unlimited-on-demand with a cap of 30.
- **Expense heads were hardcoded.** July used *Spoon, Pencil Battary, Electric Materials, Cold Drinks, Chula, Generator Parts, Burnish Mistri* and more — none of which a developer would have guessed. Heads are now editable, and rows are stored against stable keys so renaming one never moves money.
